Why sewers back up in heavy rain.

In a combined system, stormwater and household sewage share one pipe. When rain surges, the system can overflow, and the state runs a notification system for combined sewer overflows. That same pressure can push water back toward low drains in basements.

A backflow valve may reduce the risk, and a clean sewer line cleaning keeps your own lateral clear so water has a path out. Ask us whether a valve suits your house.

If your basement backs up during a storm, the cause may be the public system or your own lateral, and only a camera and a look at the cleanout can tell which. Call the 24/7 line even at night, because standing sewage should not sit.

Sump pumps and wet basements.

Not every wet basement is a sewer problem. Groundwater can seep through floors and walls after storms, and a sump pump moves it out. Test the pump before heavy weather and consider a battery backup, because the pump stops when the power does.

The village offers a rain barrel incentive through the utilities authority, per the village website. Barrels reduce roof runoff, but they do not solve a flooded basement.

Waterproofing that holds.

If damp walls and floor cracks keep returning, interior drainage or sealing can help. Basement waterproofing starts by finding where water enters, whether through a wall crack, the joint where wall meets floor, or a floor drain. We will tell you what a fix can and cannot do.

Fresh paint over a damp wall only hides the symptom. If a basement stays damp days after the rain stops, the water source is probably still active, so ask for a look before you finish the room.

Gutters and downspouts matter too. Water dumped next to the foundation ends up in the same basement, so point downspouts well away from the wall before blaming the pipes.

What a sewer backup visit looks like.

Here is how a backup call usually unfolds, so you know what to expect.

  1. 1

    We ask where it shows.

    A single toilet suggests a branch line. Every drain suggests the main or the lateral.

  2. 2

    We find the cleanout.

    A cleanout is a capped access pipe in the basement or yard that lets us reach the main without removing a fixture.

  3. 3

    We clear the line.

    A cable or water jet cuts through roots and grease and restores flow.

  4. 4

    We camera the pipe.

    The picture shows whether a crack, offset or belly caused the problem, and whether it will return.
