Failed tank and flooding: what to do first.

A tank that has failed can release dozens of gallons quickly. These steps limit the damage while we get to you.

  1. 1

    Cut the water to the tank.

    Turn the cold supply valve on the pipe above the heater clockwise until it stops. If the valve is stuck, close the main shutoff instead.

  2. 2

    Cut power or gas.

    Turn off the breaker for an electric heater. For gas, set the control knob to off. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

  3. 3

    Protect what you can.

    Move boxes and electronics off the floor and open a floor drain or door if water is spreading. Do not walk through standing water near an electrical panel.

  4. 4

    Call the 24/7 line.

Do you need a replacement or just a repair?.

A leak from the steel shell cannot be repaired. Water coming out of the tank body means the inner liner has corroded through, and replacement is the only fix. Leaks from a drain valve, fitting or relief valve are different, and our water heater repair team handles those.

Age and condition matter too. A unit that needs repeated parts, has a rusted base or shows heavy sediment may cost more to keep alive than to replace. We will tell you honestly which side of the line yours falls on.

No hot water at all: replacement or a quick fix?.

Before we write off the heater, we test the basics: power at the breaker, the pilot or ignition on gas units, and elements and thermostats on electric ones. Sometimes the unit is fine and a single part has failed.

If the tank is leaking, rusted or past reasonable repair, we move on to sizing and installing a new one. Either way you get the decision laid out before any work begins.

Choosing the replacement: tank, tankless or heat pump.

The right unit depends on household size, fuel, space and budget. This table shows how the main choices differ.

Type.Strengths.Trade-offs.
Standard gas tank.Familiar, fast recovery, works with existing gas line.Needs venting, takes floor space, stores hot water.
Standard electric tank.Simple installation, no venting.Slower recovery, needs adequate circuit capacity.
Heat pump (hybrid) tank.Draws heat from surrounding air, so electricity use drops.Needs clearance and airflow, produces condensate, taller unit.
Tankless.Compact, hot water on demand, no standby tank.Higher upfront work, scale control and venting or power needs.

Moving to tankless is a bigger decision than swapping tanks, so we cover it on its own page. See tankless water heater service before you commit.

Heat pump water heaters: worth a look.

A heat pump water heater pulls warmth from the air around it instead of making heat directly, much like a refrigerator in reverse. It needs open space, since it cools the room slightly as it works, and a path for condensate to drain away.

These units can suit a basement or utility room with room to breathe. They are not the answer for a tight closet. We check your clearance, electrical supply and drain path first, and if a standard tank fits better we say so.

Hard water and your next tank.

The Veolia Water New Jersey 2025 report lists hardness at 210 ppm as calcium carbonate, which it calls very hard. For a water heater that means sediment and scale build up on the tank floor or heat exchanger. Flushing, anode rod checks and a clean installation reduce the effect, and we will go over a simple maintenance plan when we finish.

Permits and inspection in Palisades Park.

The borough building department sets plumbing permit fees by the number of fixtures or appliances involved, so a single water heater swap is counted accordingly. For emergency repairs, the permit must be obtained within 72 hours of the work. Plumbing inspections are held Tuesday and Thursday mornings, and final inspections must be completed or penalties can follow.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Palisades Park Building Department for your exact situation. Work must be done by a licensed master plumber or an authorized contractor, and you can ask for the license number and check it with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs.

What affects the price of a replacement.

A new heater is a bigger job than a repair, and several things move the final figure.

Type and size of unit.

A standard tank, a heat pump model and a tankless unit differ widely in equipment and labor.

Venting and fuel connections.

Gas units need venting and gas lines in good order. Changing fuel adds work.

Access and removal.

Stairs, tight closets and long carries take extra time.

Code-related parts.

Required safety devices, valves and fittings are added to match the installation.
