While you wait, close the main shutoff inside the house, stop using water, and keep children and pets away from soft, flooded ground.

The water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the street main to your home. It can crack, corrode, shift or pull apart at a joint. Repairs range from a spot fix to replacing the run, and the right choice depends on what we find.

Is your water pressure suddenly low?.

Pressure that drops at every faucet at once, a trickle in the shower, or water that sputters can all trace back to a problem on the supply side. It may be a leak in the buried line, a partly closed valve, a clogged filter or a pressure regulator that has failed. The technical name for the fix when the problem is the buried pipe is water service line repair.

Low pressure on one fixture is usually local to that fixture. Low pressure everywhere is where we start looking outside the walls.

Low water pressure: where we look first.

Not every pressure problem is a broken line. We rule out the simple causes first.

Main shutoff partly closed.

A valve left half open after earlier work can choke flow to the whole house.

Pressure regulator wear.

If your home has a regulator, a worn one can let pressure sag or swing. We test it with a gauge.

Clogged fixtures or filters.

Scale in aerators, cartridges and screens reduces flow at the tap, not in the line.

Leak in the buried line.

Water escaping before it reaches the house lowers pressure for every fixture.

Corroded or narrowed pipe.

Old steel pipe can build up rust inside, shrinking the opening water flows through.

Water service line emergency: what to do first.

If water is surfacing or the meter will not stop, act now.

  1. 1

    Close the main shutoff.

    Turn the valve inside the house clockwise. If it is stuck, do not force it, and tell us when you call.

  2. 2

    Check the meter.

    With the main closed and everything off, see whether the meter still moves. That points to where the leak sits.

  3. 3

    Keep clear of flooded ground.

    Soft soil near a leaking line can collapse. Keep people and pets away, and stay off wet areas near power.

  4. 4

    Note what you saw.

    Wet spots, sunken ground, noises and bill changes help us decide where to start.

  5. 5

    Call the 24/7 line.

    We schedule after-hours and same-day visits when available.

Finding a leak in a buried line.

You cannot see a buried pipe, so we use acoustic listening, pressure tests and meter checks to locate the break before any digging. A line that holds pressure with the house isolated is probably healthy, while one that bleeds down points to a leak in the run. Our leak detection methods narrow the location so the excavation is limited.

Digging blindly is the expensive way. Locating first keeps the trench small and your landscaping intact where possible.

The meter and a high water bill.

Your meter shows whether water is moving through the line. Close every tap and appliance and watch the low-flow indicator. If it keeps turning, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures, and the buried line is a prime suspect.

Tell us what you saw and when your bill started climbing. That timeline helps us decide whether to look inside the house or out at the line.

Lead and your service line.

Veolia's 2025 water quality report for the Hackensack system discusses lead in service lines and in household plumbing, and it advises flushing taps that have been stagnant. We do not test water or diagnose health effects. If you want to know the material of your service line, ask Veolia, and ask them where their responsibility ends and yours begins.

If we expose your line during a repair, we will tell you what material we see so you can make an informed decision about replacement.

What affects the price.

Service line jobs vary more than most plumbing. These factors decide most of the cost.

Depth and length of the run.

A short, shallow line is quicker to reach than a long, deep one.

Surface above the pipe.

Lawn, driveway, sidewalk and street surfaces need different restoration work.

Pipe material.

Old steel, copper and plastic lines are repaired and replaced differently.

Spot repair or replacement.

Fixing one break costs less than renewing the full run.

Timing and access.

Emergency visits, locating work and permit steps affect the schedule and the total.

Insurance may cover some damage, but many policies exclude underground lines, so check yours. We cannot say your exact cost before seeing the job. Want your real number? Call the 24/7 line.
