Tankless Heater Size for a Family of 6 in Palisades Park, NJ

A family of six does not need a number based on headcount. It needs a flow rate that covers the busiest morning in your house.

What size tankless water heater for a family of 6 in Palisades Park, NJ? Most large households land in the range of a high-output gas unit, rated around 9 to 11 gallons per minute (GPM) at a typical winter temperature rise, though the exact answer depends on how many fixtures run at once.

Tankless water heaters are sized by flow, not by tank gallons. Add up the fixtures that run together at your peak, work out how hot the water must be after the incoming water gets heated, and compare that to the maker's rating chart.

Step one: count your peak flow.

Peak flow is the most hot water you will ask for at the same moment. In a house with six people, that is usually a weekday morning: two showers, a kitchen faucet, and a dishwasher or washing machine.

Most showerheads sold today use about 2.5 GPM or less, a kitchen faucet often uses 1.5 to 2.2 GPM, and a dishwasher or clothes washer draws a smaller amount of hot water. Check the label on your fixtures, since older ones can run higher.

Add them up. Two showers at 2.5 plus a kitchen faucet at 1.5 equals 6.5 GPM. If a third shower might run too, you are closer to 9 GPM. That number is what the heater has to keep up with.

Step two: find the temperature rise.

Temperature rise is the gap between the incoming cold water and the hot water you want at the tap. If incoming water is near 45 F in winter and you want 120 F, the heater has to add 75 degrees.

A tankless unit gives its highest flow at a small rise and less at a big one. A model rated for 10 GPM at a 50 degree rise may only deliver about 6 or 7 GPM at 75 degrees. That is why the same heater can feel fine in July and weak in January.

Use the maker's flow-versus-rise chart with your winter number, not the headline rating on the box.

Sizing options for a six-person home.

These are general planning points, not a quote. A technician should confirm against the exact model.

Peak use at home.Rough flow needed.What it usually points to.
One shower plus one faucet.About 4 to 5 GPM.A mid-size gas unit or a large electric unit may keep up.

Two smaller heaters can also work for a big household. Each serves part of the house, and one can keep running if the other needs service.

Gas or electric for a large family.

For six people, the fuel type often decides more than the brand.

Gas.

Gas units can reach high flows, but need adequate gas supply, correct pipe size, and proper venting. Gas line and vent upgrades are common surprises.

Electric.

Large electric models draw heavy current. Your electrical panel capacity and available breaker space can limit what fits.

Dual units.

Splitting the load across two heaters can solve a flow or panel problem, at the cost of more equipment to maintain.

Existing tank.

If your gas line or vent cannot handle a big tankless, a tank or a hybrid setup may be the more practical fit.

What to check before you buy.

This short list keeps a purchase from turning into a repair call after the install.

  1. 1

    Read your fixture labels.

    Note the GPM rating on each showerhead and faucet. Replace any that are unusually high flow.

  2. 2

    Check the gas meter and line.

    Ask the installer to confirm the gas meter, regulator, and pipe run can supply a larger input rating.

  3. 3

    Plan the vent path.

    The old tank flue is rarely right for a tankless. A new vent path may need new wall or roof work.

  4. 4

    Ask about water treatment.

    In very hard water areas, plan for scale. A flush valve kit and a service plan help the heater last.

  5. 5

    Confirm permit steps.

    Permit rules vary, so ask the Palisades Park Building Department before work starts.

When tank storage is the better call.

Tankless is not always the best fit for a family of six. If you cannot bring a large enough gas line or vent to the unit, or the panel cannot carry an electric model, a tank may be simpler and cheaper to keep running.
