When a leaking water heater is an emergency.

Not every drip is a disaster, but a tank that is actively leaking is. Water from the tank body itself usually means the steel liner has corroded through, and that cannot be patched. Water from a pipe fitting, the drain valve or the temperature and pressure relief valve is often fixable.

Look closely before you decide. Dry the area with a towel, then check where the new water appears. If it comes from the top, the inlet and outlet connections are the first suspects. If it comes from the bottom edge of the jacket, the tank may be done.

If water is spreading toward finished flooring, a furnace or an electrical panel, treat it as an emergency water heater repair call and phone the 24/7 line right away. We offer same-day appointments when available, and after-hours dispatch for active leaks. Signs your water heater needs attention.

Most failures announce themselves first. These are the signals we hear about most, and each points toward a different part.

Water around the base.

Check the fittings, the drain valve and the relief valve discharge pipe before blaming the tank. A leak from the shell itself means replacement.

Lukewarm or no hot water.

On electric units a burned-out element or tripped high-limit switch is common. On gas units look for a pilot that will not stay lit.

Rumbling or popping from the tank.

Mineral sediment on the bottom traps steam bubbles as the burner or element heats. That sound is the tank telling you it needs flushing.

Rusty or brown hot water.

If only the hot side runs rusty, the inside of the tank or a spent anode rod may be the source. Cold water that runs clear points away from the pipes.

Rotten egg smell from hot taps.

A reaction between the anode rod and bacteria in the tank can cause it. We can often fix it by changing the rod and flushing the tank.

Why hard water is hard on tanks here.

The Veolia Water New Jersey 2025 water quality report lists hardness in the Hackensack system at 210 ppm as calcium carbonate, which the report classes as very hard. Hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium, and heating water makes those minerals drop out as scale and sediment.

In a tank, that material settles on the bottom, insulates the water from the burner or element, and makes the heater work harder to deliver the same temperature. That is why a water heater in Palisades Park can start rumbling or running out of hot water sooner than the same model in a soft-water town. We do not put a number of years on it, because usage and installation matter too.

The practical answer is maintenance. Draining some water from the tank, checking the anode rod and testing the relief valve once a year keep sediment from piling up. If you have never flushed your tank, call and we will tell you honestly whether it is worth doing or whether the unit is past that point.

The anode rod and sediment: the two parts people forget.

Inside every steel tank water heater is an anode rod, usually magnesium or aluminum. It is designed to corrode first so the tank wall does not. Once the rod is eaten away, the steel is next, which is why checking it is cheap insurance against a leak.

Sediment is the other half of the story. A thick layer on the bottom can clog the drain valve, so a flush that should take minutes becomes a repair job. If the rod is gone and the tank is badly caked, we will say so and explain whether a repair is worth the money or a new tank makes more sense. Our notes on replacing a failed tank cover that decision in more detail.

No hot water: what usually causes it.

A cold shower can come from a simple fault. A tripped breaker, a pilot that blew out, or a single failed element can leave the tank running cold. We start with power and fuel, then the control parts, and only then talk about the tank.

If your house shares a heater between a kitchen and bathrooms, check that other taps are also cold before you call. That tells us whether the problem is the heater or one fixture's mixing valve.

Repair or replace: honest trade-offs.

A simple part failure on a tank that is otherwise sound is usually worth repairing. A leaking shell, heavy corrosion or repeated failures point toward replacement. We will not push a new unit when a thermostat will do the job.

If you are weighing a change of fuel or a heat pump model, our team can walk through the installation options once the immediate problem is handled. Landlords and managers can also ask us about multi-unit buildings, since a shared heater affects every tenant.
