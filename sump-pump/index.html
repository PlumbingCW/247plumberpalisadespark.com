A sump pump that stops while water is rising is an emergency, and our 24/7 emergency dispatch covers sump pump repair in Palisades Park, NJ. Before we arrive, stay out of standing water near outlets, extension cords or a panel, do not touch the pump while it is plugged in and wet, and move stored items off the floor if you can do it safely.

A sump pump sits in a pit at the lowest point of a basement or crawl space and pushes collected water out through a discharge pipe. An ejector pump is a related unit that lifts sewage and wastewater from fixtures below the sewer line.

Water in the basement and the pump is not running?.

If the basement floor is wet and you hear nothing from the pit, the pump may have lost power, jammed, or worn out. A float switch that sticks or a clogged inlet screen can stop an otherwise healthy motor. In some cases the pump runs but cannot push water because the discharge line is frozen, kinked or blocked.

The technical name for what we do is sump pump service: we test the switch, the motor and the check valve, then repair or replace what failed. If the water is not from the pump system at all, we say so and point you to the right fix.

Sump pump failure: what to do right now.

Safety comes first when water and electricity share a basement.

  1. 1

    Check the power safely.

    If the floor is dry around the outlet, check the breaker and the GFCI reset. Do not stand in water to do it.

  2. 2

    Do not unplug a pump standing in water.

    If the cord or outlet is wet, leave it and call. Shutting off the breaker from a dry spot is safer.

  3. 3

    Lift valuables off the floor.

    Move boxes, electronics and furniture legs out of the water path.

  4. 4

    Call the 24/7 line.

    Tell us what you hear, what you see in the pit, and whether the power is out. Same-day appointments when available can cover non-active leaks.

Sump pump or ejector pump.

The two names get mixed up. They do different jobs.

Feature.Sump pump.Ejector pump.
What it moves.Groundwater and rain seepage.Sewage and wastewater from below-grade fixtures.
Where it sits.A pit near the lowest point of the floor.A sealed, vented basin connected to a basement bathroom or laundry.
Discharge.Pipe to the outdoors.Pipe to the sewer line.
Passing solids.Small particles only.Designed for solids.
Common failure.Stuck float, burned motor, clogged inlet.Jammed impeller, failed check valve, float issue.

If your pump is tied to a basement bathroom, tell us that on the call so we bring the right unit.

Repair or replace the pump.

A pump with a stuck float or a clogged intake is often a repair job. A motor that smells burned, a cracked housing, or a unit that cycles constantly and runs hot is more likely a replacement. A pump that has run for many years may fail again soon after a repair.

We explain the trade-off honestly. Repair is usually cheaper in the moment, but replacement may avoid another failure on the next big storm. If you are building a new basement bathroom or finishing a space, see our plumbing installation page, since a new ejector setup may be involved.

Battery backup and why power outages matter.

Heavy storms and power outages often arrive together, which is when a pump matters most. A battery backup system adds a second pump or a controller with a battery that takes over when the main pump loses power or fails. A water alarm can also warn you when the pit is filling beyond its normal level.

Backup units need maintenance. Batteries lose capacity over time, and a backup pump that has never been tested may not start when needed. We can show you how to test the system and how often to check it.

Discharge and where the water goes.

A pump that works but sends water to the wrong place causes its own trouble.

Discharge pipe route.

The line should carry water well away from the foundation, not back toward the house.

Check valve.

This one-way valve keeps water from draining back into the pit after each cycle.

Winter freezing.

An exposed discharge pipe can freeze and block, so outlet design matters.

Neighbor and sidewalk impact.

Dumping onto a neighbor's property or a walkway can create disputes and ice hazards.

Rules for connections.

Where the discharge may legally terminate is a local question. Permit rules vary, so ask the Palisades Park Building Department before changing the route.

Regional flooding context.

Neighboring Ridgefield sits at about 10 feet elevation in the Hackensack River flood plain, and Ridgefield Park has a combined sewer system that can overflow in heavy rain. We do not claim that Palisades Park basements flood, because that depends on the house. We mention it because regional storms affect drains and groundwater, and a working pump is useful insurance for any basement.
