Sewer line repair means fixing the pipe that carries waste from your house to the municipal sewer. That can be a short spot repair, a trenchless liner, or full replacement, depending on what the camera shows.

Sewer backup: what to do first.

Wastewater in the home carries bacteria, so the first goal is to limit exposure and stop adding water.

  1. 1

    Stop using water.

    Hold off on flushing, showers, laundry and the dishwasher until the line is clear or repaired.

  2. 2

    Protect yourself.

    Avoid touching the water. If you must, wear rubber gloves and boots and wash well afterward.

  3. 3

    Keep power away from the water.

    Do not wade through water near a furnace, water heater or outlets. If a basement panel is wet, stay out and call the 24/7 line.

  4. 4

    Call us and describe what you see.

    Which drains are backing up, how fast, and any smell help us decide what to bring. Same-day appointments when available can cover non-active leaks.

How we find where the line failed.

We start with a camera on a flexible cable. It travels through a cleanout or a pulled toilet and shows the pipe material, joints, sags and any break. A locator at the surface then marks where the camera head sits, so we know how deep the problem is and where the dig would be.

That footage decides everything. A single offset joint is a very different job from a pipe that has collapsed for ten feet. If you want to understand the camera step before a repair, our sewer camera inspection page walks through it.

Cracked or root-invaded pipe: spot repair.

Roots find tiny gaps at joints and grow into thick masses. After we clear the roots with a cable or a jetter, the opening is still there. A spot repair replaces only the damaged section, which can mean digging a short trench instead of replacing the entire line.

Spot repair works when the rest of the pipe is sound. If the camera shows several bad joints or a pipe that is flaking along its length, a single patch only moves the problem. We say so and compare it with longer repairs rather than sell the smallest fix.

Trenchless repair and pipe lining.

Trenchless methods fix a pipe from inside through small access points. In pipe lining, a resin-soaked felt tube is pulled into the old pipe and cured so it hardens into a new pipe within the old one. Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through while breaking the old one outward.

These methods reduce digging in yards and driveways, which matters on small Palisades Park lots where the borough covers about 1.25 square miles and space between homes is tight. They do not fit every pipe. A line with a severe belly or a collapsed section may still need an open trench. We explain why when that is the case.

Cast iron sewer pipe.

Older homes may still have cast iron drain or sewer pipe, which can rust from the inside, form rough scale that catches debris, and crack with age. A camera shows heavy scaling or flaking. Cast iron can often be lined or replaced in sections, and we match the fix to the pipe's actual condition, not its age alone.
