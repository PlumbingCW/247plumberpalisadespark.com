Low ground and a high water table.

Low ground means groundwater and rain have a short path into a basement, and a sump pump may run often. Test yours before storm season by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching the float rise and the discharge run.

Basement signs after heavy rain.

Look for these when the rain stops or the basement smells off.

Water at the floor drain.

Standing water at the drain after rain can point to a blocked lateral rather than groundwater.

A pump that never stops.

Constant running may mean a stuck float or a failing check valve.

Damp walls with white crust.

The crust is mineral residue left behind as moisture seeps through.

A sewer smell.

A sulfur or sewage odor can mean a dry trap or a cracked line.

Drains and sewer laterals.

When a main drain backs up, the cause is often roots, grease or a collapsed section. Drain cleaning with a cable or hydro-jetting clears many blockages, and a camera shows whether the pipe is sound. If the camera finds a break, sewer line repair may be needed.

We say what we see on the camera and let you decide. We will not claim a replacement is needed when a cleaning would hold.
