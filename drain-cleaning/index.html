Our 24/7 emergency dispatch covers drain cleaning in Palisades Park, NJ, and while you wait you should stop running water, keep the stopper out of the way, and keep kids and pets away from any standing water.

Drain cleaning means removing whatever is blocking a drain line, usually with a hand auger, a drum machine with a cable, or a water jet. The right tool depends on where the clog sits and what it is made of, which is why we ask a few questions before we pick one.

Drain clog emergency: what to do before we arrive.

Most clogs are not dangerous, but a backup that reaches floor level can ruin flooring fast. These steps keep the problem from growing while you wait for the crew.

  1. 1

    Stop all water use.

    Do not run the dishwasher, washer or shower. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and it may come up in the lowest drain.

  2. 2

    Skip the chemical drain opener.

    Caustic products sit in a blocked pipe and can burn skin when we open it. Tell us if you already poured some in.

  3. 3

    Keep people away from wet electrical spots.

    If water is near outlets, a power strip, a laundry machine or a panel, stay out and shut off power to that area only if you can reach the switch while dry.

  4. 4

    Note which drains are slow.

    If only the kitchen sink is blocked, the clog is local. If the tub gurgles when the toilet flushes, the blockage is deeper in the line, and that detail saves time on arrival.

Kitchen sink drain clogs.

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking grease cools inside the trap and the horizontal run behind it, then catches coffee grounds, rice and food bits until the pipe narrows to a trickle. Most kitchen sinks drain through a 1.5 inch pipe, so even a thin lining of fat makes a visible difference.

A hand auger often clears the trap arm. If the clog sits farther back, we use a cable machine through a cleanout or the trap opening. Hot water alone rarely finishes the job because grease re-hardens a few feet down the line, and a short blast of dish soap will not dissolve a plug that has been building for months.

If the sink has a disposal, we check whether the clog is in the disposal outlet or beyond it. Our garbage disposal repair service covers units that hum, jam or leak while we are on site.

Signs your drain needs cleaning.

A full clog is easy to spot. These earlier signs show up weeks before it.

Water drains slowly.

A sink or tub that takes noticeably longer than it used to is usually partly blocked by hair, soap film or grease.

Gurgling after use.

Air pushed through a narrowed pipe makes a bubbling sound, often at a neighboring drain or the toilet.

Odor from the drain.

A stale or sour smell points to organic buildup on the pipe wall that a quick flush will not remove.

More than one fixture is affected.

When the tub and toilet slow down together, the clog is probably in a shared branch or the main line.

Water backs up in a floor drain.

Basement floor drains connect low in the system, so they show a main line problem first.

Snaking, cabling and when each makes sense.

A hand-cranked auger is a coiled steel cable on a drum, best for sink traps, tub drains and short runs. A motorized drum machine carries a longer, thicker cable that can push through 50 feet or more of line and cut through roots or packed debris. Cabling is the same idea at a larger scale, with cutting heads sized to the pipe.

Cable machines open a hole in a clog, but they do not always scrape the pipe wall clean. If a line fills up again within weeks, that is the signal to look at the pipe with a camera. We can also move to hydro jetting, which uses high-pressure water to scour the wall instead of just punching through.

Slow drains and the clog that is not quite a clog.

A slow drain is a partial blockage. Hair wraps around the pop-up stopper in a bathroom sink, soap scum narrows a tub drain, and toothpaste and lotion build a film in the trap. Often a quick pull of the stopper and a short auger pass is enough.

If a slow drain returns every month or two, something upstream is feeding it. That might be a belly in the pipe where water pools, a shallow slope, or a partly crushed section. We do not guess. We tell you what we saw and what the options are.

A leak at the drain line.

Sometimes the drain is not blocked but leaking, from a loose slip nut under a sink, a cracked tailpiece or a failed joint. Water under the cabinet or a damp ceiling below a bathroom are the clues. We can often fix the connection during the same visit, and if the leak is inside a wall we will say so before opening anything.

What affects the price of drain cleaning.

Drain cleaning is not a flat-rate job because the clog, the access and the pipe all vary. We give you a clear price before work starts.

Location of the clog.

A trap under a sink is quick. A clog 40 feet down a main line takes longer and more equipment.

Access to the line.

A cleanout makes the job faster. If there is no cleanout, we may have to work through a toilet flange or a roof vent.

Tool needed.

A hand auger, a drum machine and a jetter involve different equipment and labor.

What is in the pipe.

Hair and soap are simple. Roots, grease buildup or a collapsed section add time or change the plan.

Time of the call.

After-hours and weekend calls can cost more than a scheduled daytime visit.
