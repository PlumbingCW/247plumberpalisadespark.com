What does tankless gas water heater replacement cost in Palisades Park, NJ? We do not publish a price here, because an honest number depends on the house. Swapping one tankless gas unit for a similar model in the same spot is a smaller job than converting from a tank, and the gap between those two jobs is large.

Four things move the price most: the size of the new unit, the vent run, the gas line, and the condition of what is already there. The sections below walk through each so you can compare quotes fairly.

Swap-in-place versus a new setup.

A like-for-like swap means the new tankless unit uses the same wall location, vent, gas pipe, and water connections as the old one. This is the simplest case, though a different brand may still need adapters or a different bracket.

A conversion from a tank is different. The old flue is usually the wrong size for a tankless, the unit needs a wall location with room for clearances, and the condensate from a high-efficiency model needs a drain. Each of those adds labor and parts.

Ask any contractor which of these two jobs they are quoting. A low number for a conversion that skips the vent work is a warning sign.

What drives the final price.

These are the main cost factors on a tankless gas job, in the order they tend to matter.

Unit size and brand.

A higher flow rating and a condensing design cost more up front. Match the size to your peak demand, not to a sales pitch.

Venting.

High-efficiency units use plastic vent pipe, while others need stainless. Longer runs, roof penetrations, and wall cuts add labor.

Gas line capacity.

A big tankless draws more gas than a tank. If the line is undersized, it may need upsizing.

Condensate and drain.

Condensing models make acidic water that needs a drain, and sometimes a neutralizer.

Water side extras.

Isolation valves, a flush kit, a recirculation loop, or an expansion tank where required add parts and time.

Questions to ask every contractor.

Use the same list with each estimate so you are comparing like with like.

  1. 1

    Ask for the model and rating.

    Get the exact model number and the GPM it delivers at your winter temperature rise.

  2. 2

    Ask about venting.

    Which pipe material, how long a run, and where does it exit the building.

  3. 3

    Ask about the gas line.

    Does the existing pipe carry the new unit's input, or does it need upsizing.

  4. 4

    Ask who pulls the permit.

    A licensed contractor should handle the permit and the inspection. Never agree to skip it.

  5. 5

    Ask what is excluded.

    Flush kit, recirculation, disposal of the old unit, and wall repair may or may not be included.

Gas safety during any replacement.

If you smell gas at any time, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Do not turn switches on or off, and do not call a plumber from inside.

A gas appliance should only be disconnected and reconnected by a licensed plumber who tests the joints after work. Ask what test they run, and ask for a printed record. For the unit itself, see our page on tankless water heater service.
