Fix a Leaking Outdoor Faucet in Palisades Park, NJ

A dripping hose bib wastes water and can damage the wall behind it. Most drips come down to a cheap worn part, but not all.

Written by the Palisades Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026.

To repair a leaking outdoor faucet, first find where the water comes out. A drip from the spout usually means a worn washer or seat. A leak around the handle usually means worn packing. Water inside the house when the faucet is off points to a cracked pipe behind the wall, which is not a DIY job.

This guide walks through the safe checks and repairs a homeowner in Palisades Park, NJ can do with basic tools, and the point where it makes sense to call a plumber.

Find where the leak is coming from.

Run the faucet briefly, then shut it off and watch for a minute. The location of the drip tells you the part.

Drip from the spout.

The washer or seat inside the valve is worn, so the valve no longer seals when closed.

Water around the handle.

The packing nut or packing washer under the handle is worn or loose.

Leak where the faucet meets the house.

A loose connection, a failed seal, or a pipe problem behind the siding.

Drip only with the hose attached.

The hose washer is flat or cracked. This is the cheapest fix of all.

Wet wall or basement ceiling inside.

Water may be escaping behind the wall. Stop DIY work and shut off the supply.

Quick fix: check the hose washer first.

Unscrew the hose and look at the rubber washer inside the female coupling. If it is flat, cracked, or missing, replace it. They are inexpensive and sold at any hardware store.

A dripping hose connection is the most common complaint and takes about two minutes to fix. Hand tighten the hose after replacing the washer, since overtightening can crush the new one.

Before you touch the faucet.

Find the indoor shutoff valve for the outdoor line, often in the basement or utility area near the wall where the faucet sits. Close it and open the outdoor faucet to drain the line. If you cannot find a shutoff, use the main water valve. Never take a faucet apart while it is under pressure.

How to replace worn packing.

This fixes a leak around the handle on a standard compression hose bib. You need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver, and replacement packing or a packing washer.

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain.

    Close the indoor shutoff and open the faucet until it stops dripping.

  2. 2

    Remove the handle.

    Take out the screw in the center of the handle and lift the handle off.

  3. 3

    Snug the packing nut.

    Sometimes a quarter turn clockwise on the packing nut stops the leak. Turn the water back on to test.

  4. 4

    Replace the packing if it still leaks.

    Shut the water off again, unscrew the packing nut, and replace the old packing washer or string with a matching one.

  5. 5

    Reassemble and test.

    Tighten by hand plus a small turn with the wrench, open the shutoff slowly, and check for drips.

How to replace the washer and seat.

If water drips from the spout, shut off the supply and remove the stem by unscrewing the bonnet nut. At the bottom of the stem there is a rubber washer held by a small screw. Swap it for one of the same size and shape.

If the metal seat inside the body is pitted, a new washer will not seal for long. Seat repair needs special tools. At that point replacing the whole faucet is usually the cleaner option.

If your indoor shutoff does not close fully, stop. Forcing an old valve can break it and turn a small drip into a flood.

Winter freezing and the wall behind the faucet.

A hose left attached through a cold snap traps water in the faucet. Freezing water expands and can split the body or the pipe behind it. The faucet may look fine all winter and then leak inside the wall once it thaws.

Disconnect hoses in fall, close the indoor shutoff if there is one, and open the outdoor spigot to drain it. A frost-proof sillcock is built with a long stem so the seat sits inside the heated space, but it must slope slightly down to drain.

Signs the leak is bigger than the faucet.

Watch for a stain on the interior wall, a musty smell near the faucet, or a water meter that keeps moving when everything is off. These point to a hidden leak. Our leak detection service uses listening gear and pressure tests to find where water is escaping without opening more wall than needed.
