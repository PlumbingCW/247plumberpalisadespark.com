Commercial plumbing means the supply, drain, grease, backflow and fixture systems in restaurants, retail units, offices and multifamily buildings. The scale and the rules differ from a house, so we plan around your hours and your equipment.

When a commercial plumbing failure is an emergency.

Treat it as urgent when sewage or grease is coming up through floor drains, water is flowing near electrical equipment, a pipe has burst, or a fixture cannot be shut off. Health departments and landlords care about these events, and customers notice them fast. If you cannot operate safely, stop operating and call.

We take after-hours and weekend calls, because kitchens and shops often hit trouble during the busiest shifts.

Grease traps and kitchen drain lines.

Restaurants send fat, oil and grease down the drain, where it cools and builds up. A grease trap or interceptor catches much of it, but only if it is cleaned on schedule. A neglected trap lets grease reach the building drain, and that is where backups begin.

We clean traps, clear grease-clogged lines and use hydro jetting to scour pipe walls when snaking leaves residue behind. Check with your property manager and local authorities on cleaning frequency, since requirements can vary.

Backflow testing and prevention.

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the clean supply. Many commercial buildings have them on irrigation, boilers, fire lines or kitchen equipment. These devices have to be tested to be trusted.

Testing requirements, schedules and who must be certified depend on the device and local rules, so confirm with the borough and your water supplier. We can discuss what your equipment needs, and we will say clearly if a certified tester or a different specialist is required.

Commercial leak detection, with honest limits.

Business owners often notice a bill spike or damp ceiling before any pipe is visible. We use listening gear, thermal imaging, moisture readings and pressure tests, the same approach as our leak detection service. Large buildings with many risers and shared systems can take longer, and some jobs need building plans or managers to open spaces.

We do what we can on each visit and tell you what remains. We will not claim we can locate every leak in a large complex in a single pass.

Floor drains, restrooms and fixtures.

Everyday trouble spots in shops, offices and kitchens.

Floor drain overflow.

Water or sewage backing up at a floor drain points to a blockage downstream. Stop using sinks and flush points and call.

Restroom stoppages.

Toilets and urinals that back up repeatedly need the drain line checked, not just plunged.

Slow kitchen sinks.

A sink that drains slowly is often an early grease warning before a full backup.

Water heater trouble.

No hot water affects dishwashing and sanitation. Look at our water heater pages or call.

Leaking valves and fixtures.

Worn valves, flush mechanisms and faucets waste water and make bills unpredictable.

Permits and licensed work.

Plumbing work in NJ is limited to licensed master plumbers, authorized plumbing contractors or their W-2 employees. The license number must appear on vehicles, invoices and advertising. Permit rules vary, so ask the Palisades Park Building Department. For emergency repairs, a permit is required within 72 hours of the work, and plumbing inspections run Tuesday and Thursday mornings.
