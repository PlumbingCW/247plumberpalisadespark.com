A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, using a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger instead of a storage tank. In Palisades Park, NJ it is a popular upgrade for homes short on space, and it needs care to keep delivering. We service, repair and install these units.

If your hot water runs out, fluctuates or shows an error code, the cause is usually fixable. Call the 24/7 line to book a visit.

Hot water that goes cold or cuts out mid-shower.

That swing from hot to cold to hot again has a name: the cold-water sandwich. It happens when a tankless heater shuts off between short uses, and the pipe holds a slug of cold water before warm water arrives. Mineral scale inside the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet filter or a minimum flow rate that is not being met can cause the cut-outs too.

These symptoms point to a tankless water heater that needs flushing, adjusting or repair. The sections below cover each one.

Why 210 ppm hardness matters for a tankless unit.

The Veolia Water New Jersey 2025 report lists hardness at 210 ppm as calcium carbonate, which it classes as very hard. A tankless unit heats water quickly through narrow passages, so mineral scale can narrow those passages and reduce flow and efficiency.

Scale is the main enemy of a tankless heater in Palisades Park. We do not give a lifespan number, since usage varies, but regular descaling is the best defense. A water softener may also help, and we can discuss that without making health claims about your water.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater.

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger and then rinses it clean. This is the most common service call on tankless units.

  1. 1

    Isolate the unit.

    We turn off gas or power and close the water supply. Service valves on the unit make the flush straightforward.

  2. 2

    Circulate descaler.

    A pump moves a mild acid solution through the exchanger for the time the manufacturer specifies.

  3. 3

    Rinse and clean the filter.

    We flush with clear water and clean the inlet screen, which catches debris before it can restrict flow.

  4. 4

    Test and record.

    The unit is restarted, temperature and flow are checked, and any error history is reviewed.

Common tankless repairs.

Tankless units fail in recognizable ways. Here is what we usually find.

Scaled heat exchanger.

Reduced flow, fluctuating temperature or overheating errors often trace back to scale.

Clogged inlet filter.

A blocked screen starves the unit of flow and can trigger low-flow behavior.

Flame or ignition faults.

On gas models, a weak flame sensor, igniter or condensate problem can stop heating.

Element or sensor failure.

On electric models, a failed element, thermistor or flow sensor can leave water lukewarm.

Venting and combustion air.

Blocked or damaged venting can lock out a gas unit for safety.

Error codes: what they mean in general.

Most tankless units display a numeric code when something goes wrong. Codes differ by brand, and the manual defines them, so we do not guess from memory. In general they point to ignition, flow, temperature, venting or sensor problems.

Write down the code and brand before you call. Do not reset the unit repeatedly, since some faults involve combustion safety. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

Tankless installation and conversion.

Installing a tankless unit is a bigger job than swapping a tank. A gas model may need a larger gas line and new venting. An electric model may need a new circuit and panel capacity. We also look at whether you want service valves and a recirculation setup to reduce wait times.

Converting from a tank usually means removing the old heater, rerouting pipes and mounting the new unit on a wall. If you are still deciding, read about water heater installation for comparison, and see replacement if your tank has already failed.
