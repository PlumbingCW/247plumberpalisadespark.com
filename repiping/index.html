Repiping means replacing a home's worn water supply pipes with new ones, either a section at a time or throughout the house. In Palisades Park, NJ, it comes up when old galvanized or aging copper lines leak repeatedly, run rusty or lose pressure. Not every old pipe needs replacing, and we say so when a repair will do.

We start by finding out what you actually have and where it is failing. Then we lay out repair and replacement choices with their trade-offs, so you decide with real information.

Brown water and weak pressure: what your pipes may be telling you.

Rust-colored water on first use, a shower that has slowly weakened, or a new pinhole leak every year are classic signs that old supply pipes are wearing out. People often search for a fix by asking how to replace galvanized pipes or whether a whole-house repipe is worth it. The service that answers both is repiping.

The cause is rarely a single bad spot. Steel pipes corrode from the inside, and the problem tends to spread along the whole run.

Signs it may be time to replace.

One symptom alone does not mean you need new pipes. A pattern does.

Repeated pinhole leaks.

Fixing one leak and finding another a few feet away often means the pipe wall is thinning along the run.

Discolored water.

Rust-brown or yellow water, especially after the water has sat, can come from corroding steel pipe.

Falling pressure.

Mineral and rust buildup narrows the inside of old pipes and restricts flow to upstairs fixtures first.

Visible corrosion.

Green crust on copper joints or flaking rust on steel are signs of advanced wear.

Frequent repair calls.

When patches are adding up, the total can approach the cost of a planned replacement.

How we figure out what needs replacing.

We look at the pipe material, trace the layout, check pressure at several fixtures and inspect accessible runs in basements and utility spaces. We do not assume. Homes in an older borough like Palisades Park, incorporated in 1899, can have a mix of pipe materials from different eras, so we look at your actual pipes rather than guess from the age of the neighborhood.

Where a leak is hidden, leak detection can confirm the problem spot before anyone decides on a bigger project.

Repair a section or repipe.

The honest answer depends on how widespread the wear is.

Situation.Better fit.Why.
A single leak, rest of pipe in good shape.Spot repair.Lower cost and little disruption.
Repeated leaks on one run.Replace that run.Stops the cycle on the weakest section.
Rusty water and low pressure throughout.Whole-house repipe.Addresses the cause across the system.

We will not recommend a full repipe when a smaller fix solves your problem.

PEX or copper: choosing the new pipe.

Both are common choices for supply lines. Each has trade-offs, and your layout matters.

Feature.PEX.Copper.
Material.Flexible plastic tubing.Rigid metal pipe.
Installation.Bends around corners, fewer joints, often less wall opening.Needs fittings at every turn and soldering.
Corrosion.Does not rust.Can corrode over time depending on conditions.
Sunlight and heat.Not for outdoor or sunlit exposure.Handles exposed runs.
Where it fits.Many whole-house and section replacements.Exposed runs or where a customer prefers metal.

Local rules and manufacturer instructions govern what can be used where. We confirm that before we quote.

Copper pipe repair without replacing everything.

Copper lasts a long time, and a single failure does not mean the whole system is finished. Pinholes, split seams and failed solder joints are often fixed by replacing a short section. We check the neighboring pipe and fittings before closing the wall, because a thin spot nearby is a reason to replace more.

If the same run has failed more than once, we say so, and we show you the pipe so the decision is based on what we see.

Lead guidance and household plumbing.

The 2025 Veolia water quality report for the Hackensack system, which serves Palisades Park, discusses lead in service lines and in household plumbing. It also advises flushing taps after water has been standing. We do not test water or diagnose health effects. If lead is a concern, contact Veolia customer service and ask about testing options and records for your address.

When we open a wall, we tell you what pipe material we find so you know what your home has.

What affects the price.

Repiping cost depends on the house more than on any formula. These are the factors that decide it.

Size and layout.

More bathrooms, floors and fixtures mean more pipe and more connections.

Material choice.

PEX and copper differ in material and labor, and fittings add up.

Wall and floor access.

Open framing is quick. Finished walls, tile and plaster take more careful work.

Scope.

Replacing one run is a smaller job than replacing the whole system.

Permits and finish repairs.

Permit fees based on fixtures, inspections, and patching drywall are separate line items.
