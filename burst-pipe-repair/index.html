A burst pipe sends gallons of water into walls and floors every minute, so the first job is stopping it. Our team provides 24/7 emergency dispatch across Palisades Park, NJ. While you wait, close the main water shutoff, open a low faucet to relieve pressure, and keep everyone away from wet outlets, appliances and the electrical panel.

A burst pipe is any supply or drain line that has split, cracked or separated and is letting water escape. Causes range from freezing and corrosion to a failed joint or a worn shutoff valve. The fix may be a short patch or a longer section replacement, and we explain which one fits before we start.

What to do in the first five minutes.

Speed matters more than perfection. Do these in order, and call us as soon as water is under control.

  1. 1

    Find and close the main valve.

    It is usually where the water line enters the house, often near the meter or the water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

  2. 2

    Drain the pressure.

    Open the lowest faucet or a tub tap so the leftover water in the pipes drains out and the spray slows.

  3. 3

    Stay out of wet electrical areas.

    Never touch outlets, cords or the panel while standing in water. If you cannot reach the breaker dry, leave it and call.

  4. 4

    Contain what you can.

    Use buckets and towels, and lift furniture and valuables off the floor. Take photos for your insurance file.

  5. 5

    Call the 24/7 line.

    Tell us where the water is coming from and what you have shut off. We schedule same-day and after-hours visits when available.

When a burst pipe is an emergency.

Treat it as an emergency any time water is spraying, flowing through a ceiling, or pooling near electrical items. Even a slow leak inside a wall can ruin drywall and insulation within days. If you can stop the water at the main and nothing is wet near power, you may be able to wait for the next available appointment, but call anyway so we can plan.

Cracked, split and broken pipe: what we repair.

Pipes fail in different ways, and the repair follows the failure.

Split copper.

Freezing and age can open a seam along a copper line. We cut out the damaged section and join new pipe with proper fittings.

Failed joints.

A soldered or compression joint that lets go often sprays at the connection. Re-making the joint is usually quick if the pipe around it is sound.

Corroded steel.

Older galvanized lines rust from inside and pinhole or crack. Repeated failures usually point toward replacing a larger run.

Broken supply valves.

The small shutoff valves under sinks and toilets can crack or seize. We replace the valve and the supply line together.

Damaged drain pipe.

A cracked or separated drain line leaks wastewater into the structure. It needs prompt repair, since it is more than a nuisance.

How we find the real problem.

The visible wet spot is not always the failure point. Water follows framing and pipe runs, so we trace it back, check pressure, and look at the rest of the line for signs of the same weakness. When the break is hidden, we use moisture meters and thermal imaging before cutting anything.

If you suspect water is escaping somewhere unseen, our leak detection work locates the source with minimal opening.

Frozen pipes: before and after the burst.

Water expands as it freezes, and that pressure can split a pipe even if the ice has not yet melted. The danger is often greater after the thaw, when flow resumes through the crack. If a pipe is frozen but not yet burst, keep the faucet slightly open, warm the area gently with air from a space heater placed safely away from water, and never use an open flame.

If you find a bulge, frost on a pipe or no water at all in cold weather, shut the main before it thaws and call us. A cracked pipe found early is a small repair.

Broken shutoff valves and the main valve.

A main valve that will not close turns a small problem into a big one. Old gate valves can seize or snap, and a valve stuck open leaves you with no way to stop a flood. If your main will not turn, do not force it hard enough to break it. Call us and we will advise on the next step, which may include shutting water at the street through your water utility.

Replacing a worn main shutoff is a small job that is easier to do now than during a flood.

After the water stops.

Once the pipe is repaired, drying matters. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring can grow mold if left damp, so open windows, run fans and consider a drying service. We handle the plumbing side and do not diagnose health effects or perform mold remediation.

What affects the price.

Burst pipe repair cost varies with the break and the building. Here is what moves the number.

Pipe location and access.

An exposed pipe in a basement is faster to repair than one inside a finished wall or ceiling.

Pipe material and size.

Copper, steel and plastic need different fittings and techniques, and larger lines take more material.

Length of damaged section.

A single split is a short repair. Corroded runs may call for replacing longer lengths.

Time of the call.

After-hours, weekend and holiday visits differ from scheduled daytime work.

Follow-up repairs.

Patching drywall, painting and drying are separate steps from the plumbing repair itself.
