A sewer camera inspection lets you see the inside of a pipe you cannot otherwise reach. Our crew provides sewer camera inspection in Palisades Park, NJ for buyers, owners planning a project, and anyone chasing a repeat drain problem. The camera finds cracks, roots, sags and offsets, and a locator marks where each one sits on the surface.

Palisades Park was incorporated in 1899 and now holds 7,668 housing units, so many buildings may sit on pipe that has been underground for a long time. A camera replaces guesswork with a picture.

Why inspect before you buy an older home.

A home inspection usually does not include the underground sewer line. Replacing or lining a failing line can be one of the larger repair bills a new owner faces, and it is a cost you can know about before closing. A camera pass during a pre-purchase inspection gives you footage to share with your attorney, agent or inspector.

If the footage shows a problem, you can negotiate a repair, a credit or a different offer. If it shows clean, round pipe, you buy with more confidence. Either way you learn something that a walk-through cannot tell you.

What the camera shows.

The camera head carries a light and a lens and sends live video to a monitor. These are the findings we look for.

Pipe material.

Cast iron, clay, plastic and older fiber pipe all look different on camera and fail in different ways.

Root intrusion.

Roots enter at joints and appear as fine white threads or thick mats.

Cracks and breaks.

A longitudinal crack, a circular crack or a missing section is visible against the pipe wall.

Sags and bellies.

A low spot holds water. The camera shows a pool where the pipe should be dry.

Offset joints.

Where two sections no longer line up, debris catches and roots can enter.

Scale and buildup.

Rust scale in iron pipe and grease on the wall narrow the bore.

How the inspection works, step by step.

The visit is short and does not require digging.

  1. 1

    Find the access point.

    We use a cleanout when there is one. If not, we remove a toilet or use another accessible opening.

  2. 2

    Feed the camera.

    The cable moves toward the main sewer while we watch and record the run.

  3. 3

    Mark the surface.

    A locator tracks the camera head so we can mark depth and position above ground.

  4. 4

    Review the footage with you.

    We stop on defects, explain what they are, and discuss what they mean.

  5. 5

    Deliver the summary.

    You receive the video and a written description of what we saw.

What the report includes.

Your report lists the pipe material we saw, any defects with their approximate distance from the access point, and whether the line looked clear or blocked. It does not state a price for repairs, because we cannot know your exact cost before the next step is chosen. If something needs work, we outline the realistic options.

If the footage shows a clog, we may suggest sewer line cleaning or hydro jetting first. If it shows a broken pipe, the next conversation is about sewer line repair. Nothing is repaired during an inspection unless you ask.

Not every drain issue needs a camera. This helps you decide.

Shared and older lines.

Many two-family and multi-unit buildings in a dense borough of about 1.25 square miles share one lateral to the street. A camera can show which branch feeds which unit and where lines join. That helps owners and property managers decide who is affected by a defect.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Palisades Park Building Department if the footage leads to a repair.
