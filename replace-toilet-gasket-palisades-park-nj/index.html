How to Replace a Toilet Gasket: Palisades Park, NJ Guide

A failed toilet gasket leaks at the floor and can rot the subfloor. The fix is manageable if you go slowly.

Written by the Palisades Park 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 30, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026.

To install a toilet gasket, you shut off the water, drain the toilet, unbolt and lift it off the flange, scrape off the old wax ring, set a new gasket on the flange or the toilet horn, and press the toilet straight down. Then you bolt it, reconnect the supply, and test for leaks.

This Palisades Park, NJ guide covers the signs of a failed gasket, the tools, the steps, and the points where it makes sense to hand the job to a plumber.

Signs the gasket has failed.

The gasket, usually a wax ring, seals the toilet to the drain flange. When it fails, water or gas escapes at the floor.

Water around the base.

Moisture after flushing, not from the tank or supply line, points to the seal.

A rocking toilet.

A loose toilet can break the seal even with a good ring.

Sewer smell near the toilet.

Gas escaping past the ring. Check traps in other drains too.

Soft or stained flooring.

Slow leaks soak the subfloor. Press on the area with a screwdriver tip to feel for soft wood.

Water stain on the ceiling below.

If the toilet is on an upper floor, a seal leak can show up downstairs.

Rule out the easy causes first.

Wipe the tank, supply line, and base dry, then watch for the first drip. A sweating tank, a loose supply nut, or a leaking shutoff can look like a base leak. Check these before you pull the toilet.

If you are unsure where the water starts, our leak detection service can track a hidden source, which matters when water has reached a ceiling or wall.

Tools and materials.

Gather these before you shut the water off.

Item.Why you need it.
New wax ring or wax-free gasket.The seal itself. Match it to your drain and toilet.
New closet bolts and washers.Old ones are often corroded.
Adjustable wrench and screwdriver.For nuts, the tank, and caps.
Putty knife.To scrape old wax from the flange.
Rubber gloves and rags.Clean-up and sanitary protection.
Bucket and sponge.To drain the tank and bowl.
Rag or plug.To block the drain opening during the swap.
A helper.Toilets are heavy and awkward.

A wax-free gasket is more forgiving of a slightly uneven flange, but follow the manufacturer's directions for either type.

Step by step: replace the gasket.

Take your time. Rushing causes most repeat leaks.

  1. 1

    Shut off the water.

    Turn the small valve behind the toilet clockwise. Flush to empty the tank, then sponge out what remains.

  2. 2

    Disconnect the supply line.

    Loosen the nut by hand or wrench. Keep a bucket under it for the water left in the line.

  3. 3

    Remove the toilet.

    Pop the caps and remove the nuts on the floor bolts. Rock the toilet gently and lift it straight up with a helper, then set it on old towels or cardboard.

  4. 4

    Clean the flange.

    Scrape off old wax and stuff a rag in the opening to block sewer gas. Inspect the flange for cracks or a bent ring.

  5. 5

    Set the new gasket.

    Place the ring on the flange or on the toilet horn as the instructions say. Fit new bolts upright in their slots.

  6. 6

    Lower the toilet straight down.

    Line up the bolt holes, press down evenly, and avoid sliding the toilet sideways. Do not rock it after it sits.

  7. 7

    Secure and test.

    Tighten nuts alternately, a little at a time, so the porcelain does not crack. Reconnect the supply, open the valve, flush several times, and check the base.

Mistakes that cause repeat leaks.

Reusing an old ring, overtightening bolts, setting the toilet and then lifting it to adjust, and skipping a check of the flange all lead to leaks. A flange that sits below the finished floor or is cracked will not hold a standard ring, and needs repair first.

When to stop and call a plumber.

Broken or corroded flange.

The ring needs a solid surface. Flange repair is not a quick patch.

Soft or rotted floor.

Wood damage must be assessed before a toilet goes back.

Shutoff valve that will not close.

Forcing it can break the valve. A plumber can replace it.

Cracked toilet.

Hairline cracks leak and cannot be sealed.

You cannot lift the toilet safely.

A toilet is heavy and slippery. Do not risk a back injury.

If the toilet overflows or sewage appears.

Stop using the toilet, close the shutoff valve, and keep children and pets away. Sewage carries bacteria, so use gloves and do not touch it barehanded. Turn off power only if the breaker is dry and reachable.
