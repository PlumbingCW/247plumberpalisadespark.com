Prevent Toilet and Disposal Clogs in Palisades Park, NJ

Most toilet and disposal clogs are caused by a short list of things that should have gone in the trash. Here is the list and the habits that keep lines clear.

How do you prevent a clog in a toilet and a garbage disposal in Palisades Park, NJ? Flush only human waste and toilet paper, and grind only soft food scraps with cold water running. Wipes, paper towels, grease, fibrous peels, and coffee grounds cause most of the calls a plumber sees.

Neither fixture is built to be a trash can. A toilet trap is about two inches wide at its narrowest, and a disposal has a small grinding chamber with no way to shred stringy or sticky material. A few habits keep both running for years.

What never goes down the toilet.

Paper that says flushable on the package still does not break down like toilet paper. Treat these as trash.

Wipes of any kind.

Baby wipes, cleaning wipes, and makeup wipes hold together in the pipe and snag on rough spots, building a mat.

Paper towels and tissues.

They are made to stay strong when wet, which is the opposite of what a drain line needs.

Cotton swabs and dental floss.

Small, but they catch hair and paper and tie a clog together.

Feminine products and diapers.

These swell with water and can wedge in the trap or the main line.

Cat litter and food.

Clumping litter sets like cement in a slow section of pipe.

Disposal habits that prevent jams and clogs.

A disposal chops food into small pieces. It does not dissolve grease or clear long fibers.

  1. 1

    Run cold water first.

    Start the cold tap before the disposal and keep it running for about 15 seconds after. Cold keeps fats solid so they flush through instead of smearing.

  2. 2

    Feed it slowly.

    Add small amounts at a time. A packed chamber jams the motor and sends big lumps down the drain.

  3. 3

    Skip the problem foods.

    Potato peels, celery, corn husks, onion skins, eggshells, rice, pasta, and coffee grounds can clump or tangle.

  4. 4

    Scrape pans into the trash.

    Pour cooled grease into a jar or can and throw it out. Never pour it down the sink.

  5. 5

    Clean it monthly.

    Grind a handful of ice, then rinse. Wipe the splash guard with a brush and dish soap.

Why clogs happen in the first place.

Most toilet clogs start with too much paper in a single flush, or an object that does not break down. A low-flow toilet moves less water, so there is less force to push material along. Flush twice if you must use extra paper, or flush in stages.

Most disposal clogs come from grease and starch. Fat coats the pipe, then flour, rice, or pasta sticks to it. After a few months the drain below the disposal narrows, and the disposal hums and backs up into the sink.

If a toilet backs up and the tub or floor drain also gurgles, the blockage is in the branch or main line, not in one fixture. That is a drain-cleaning job and a camera look, and a plunger will not reach it.

Never put your hand in a disposal. Switch off the wall switch and unplug it, or use the breaker, before any work.

When it is not a quick fix.

A toilet that keeps clogging after plunging, or a disposal that smells and backs up each week, usually points to a line problem. Call for toilet repair when a bowl runs, leaks at the base, or rocks. Call for garbage disposal repair when the unit leaks, jams repeatedly, or trips the breaker.

If the water rises toward the rim, shut the supply valve behind the toilet and stop flushing. Keep family members out of any sewage-wet area.

Keep a plunger under each bathroom sink and a spare disposal hex key where you can find it. Knowing where they are before a clog hits saves a stressful search.

Dense housing in Palisades Park and shared lines.

Palisades Park covers about 1.25 square miles and had 7,668 housing units in the 2020 census, so many homes sit close together. In a building with more than one unit, a stuck wipe or a grease plug can affect more than your own fixture.

If a toilet or disposal clog keeps coming back in a multi-unit building, report it to the landlord and tell them whether neighbors have the same trouble. Bring up both the toilet and the kitchen if you see slow drains.
